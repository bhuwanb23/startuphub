**YUKTI – National Innovation Repository (NIR)** — https://yukti.mic.gov.in · https://iic.mic.gov.in
An initiative of the **Ministry of Education's Innovation Cell (MIC)** and **AICTE**. Every IIC institution is expected to use it.

**What it does (quoted from official sources):**
> "IIC institutions will **Create and Maintain a Repository of Innovations** by Identifying, Scouting and Evaluating Innovations and Start-ups Developed by Faculty Members, Students, Alumni and Incubated Start-ups on Regular Basis."
> "Indexing of Innovations... Nominations of Validated Innovations/Start-ups from NIR and IIC Institutions to take part in National Contests and Grand Challenges"
> — https://yukti.mic.gov.in

**⭐ TRL-based categorisation — the category nobody else implements:**
> "Innovators can submit their innovations under following categories:
> 1. **Idea / PoC (TRL 1-4)**
> 2. **Innovation / Prototype (TRL 5-9)**
> 3. **Business and Start-up**"
> — https://iic.mic.gov.in/assets/html/YuktiInnovationRepository.html

**⭐ Built-in verification workflow with HEI-side control:**
> "That means your institute has not verified your submission. So kindly inform the person who is handling the IIC to assign verifiers for your submission."

> — https://yukti.mic.gov.in · https://iic.mic.gov.in

**Related MIC programmes:** Institution's Innovation Council (IIC, launched 2018), School Innovation Council, Smart India Hackathon, Udyamotsav, IP Utsav, YIC/AICTE Productization Fellowship, YUKTI 2.0, NFTTIC (National Forum for Technology Transfer and Innovation Commercialization, hub-and-spoke for tech transfer).

# PART 4 — WHAT NOBODY DOES WELL

This is the core output. I ranked these by how confidently no competitor covers them, and by how painful they are for the Indian institutional buyer.

## 🔴 1. IPR / PATENT PORTFOLIO MANAGEMENT — **zero competitors**

**No vendor in this market has a real IPR module.** Not AcceleratorApp. Not Accubate. Not Incubatee. Not EcoSync. Not LabelNest. Not Gohorto.

The only two traces anywhere:
- **Incu (Sweden)** — *"Moduler med nyckeldata om avtal, **patent**, kapital, varumärke m.m."* A data field, not a workflow.
- **IncubatX** — an "IP" tile on an impact dashboard. No invention disclosure, no filing status, no ownership.

Meanwhile the Indian institutional reality is unambiguously demanding:
- ARIIA data collection **explicitly requires** "Patent Application No," "Year of Patent Application," "Current Status," "IP / Patented Technology" (verified in real institutional submissions)
- MeitY's official incubation playbook requires IP due diligence: ownership clarity, freedom to operate, defensibility, and distinguishing **ownership vs. 2-year university tech-transfer licence**
- IIT Madras ran an **IP Management Cell** covering "early-stage disclosures and prior-art evaluation to drafting, prosecution and long-term portfolio management" plus a TTO — filing 431 patents in one year

**The gap nobody has:** invention disclosure → prior-art search → provisional/specification drafting handoff → filing status and deadline tracking (Indian Patent Office + international) → prosecution → grant → annuity → **technology transfer / licensing with royalty tracking** → revenue share to the Section-8/SPV entity → co-ownership splits between institute, faculty, and startup. This is a genuine, expensive, India-specific, multi-year workflow.

**Wedge:** the closest commercial analogues — Questel, Iptizen, Dor-Kluster — are IP-docketing tools for IP lawyers, built for Western law-firm workflows, cost institutional money, and know nothing about Indian patent law, IIT/SPV equity splits, or university tech-transfer policy. **Nobody has joined IP docketing to incubation programme management.**

## 🔴 2. TRL / TECHNOLOGY READINESS LEVEL MODELLING — **zero competitors**

Every Indian platform tracks "stage" as a free-text or dropdown field. **Nobody models TRL as a first-class, reportable, government-alignable object.**

Why this is not optional in India:
- **YUKTI-NIR requires TRL bands** (Idea/PoC **TRL 1–4**; Innovation/Prototype **TRL 5–9**; Business/Startup) and routes submissions to different national programmes based on them
- **ARIIA** asks for "Current Status, Stage of Innovation (**TRL Stage**)"
- **MeitY's playbook** uses TRL as the benchmark for investment readiness
- **TIDE 2.0** and **NIDHI/BIRAC** funding tranches are tied to TRL progression

**The gap nobody has:** a TRL engine that (a) auto-suggests TRL from submission evidence, (b) tracks progression1→9 over time per startup/innovation, (c) maps a startup's current TRL to **eligible funding schemes** (BIRAC BIG at higher TRL, NIDHI-PRAYAS at lower, MeitY TIDE for deep tech, DST for prototype), and (d) **exports YUKTI-NIR-ready and ARIIA-ready records automatically.**

IncubatX claims 240+ schemes but ties matching to **sector/stage/team**, not TRL. That is the gap.

## 🔴 3. YUKTI-NIR / IIC / ARIIA SUBMISSION & VERIFICATION WORKFLOW — **zero competitors, and it is a government mandate**

Not one commercial platform has any of this. Not EcoSync, not LabelNest, not AcceleratorApp.

The reality:
- MIC's IIC model is described as working "in coordinated manner through **a robust digital platform**" with a **scoring and reward system**
- YUKTI-NIR has an HEI-side **"Add Verifier"** workflow, a **"Recommended"/"Not Recommended"** status that gates eligibility for the YUKTI Innovation Challenge, and year-round submission capability
- **Verified innovations auto-pool into ARIIA Section 4 ranking data**

This means today's HEI workflow is: maintain your startup record *in a commercial platform* → then **retype/export it** into the government portal → then hand-maintain a *third* copy for ARIIA annual submission → and for NIRF, a *fourth*.

**The gap nobody has:** a one-click, schema-mapped exporter to YUKTI-NIR (all three categories), plus a live ARIIA Section 4 tracker, plus the IIC activity calendar and scoring model, plus the Innovation Ambassador training register — **sourced from the same startup record.** This is the highest-value, most defensible feature in the entire category and it is completely open.

## 🟠 4. UNIFIED MANDATE-SPANNING REPORTING — the "four spreadsheets in a university" problem

Gohorto's own LinkedIn nails the buyer pain:
> "Universities don't just run isolated programs; they manage a complex ecosystem that includes: → **Incubator → Accelerator → Hackathon → Entrepreneurship courses → Grant programs → Industry partnerships.** Each of these areas has different teams, budgets, and timelines."

No platform lets one institution hold a **single KPI frame across all of these simultaneously**:

## 🟠 6. FACULTY, INNOVATION AMBASSADORS & THE NON-STARTUP INNOVATOR — everyone builds only for startups

**No platform in this market has a first-class faculty/innovator entity.** They all start at "startup application."

But the Indian HEI innovation ecosystem is mostly *not* startups:
- **AICTE Innovation Ambassadors** (the standard IIC cadre) — faculty and students, trained and registered, run awareness programmes. Counted by IICs.
- **Student Innovative Project (SIP)** programmes (e.g. NIT Calicut) — student projects, not companies
- **IPR Cell** workloads — disclosures, filings, and prosecution tracking for faculty inventors
- **Consultancy / sponsored research** projects with TRL1–4 outcomes
- **Institute research projects** needing TRL tracking for ARIIA

Faculty are the **source** of everything downstream (inventions → disclosures → patents → licences → startups). No platform lets a faculty member hold a profile that follows an invention from disclosure through to a licensed startup and royalty income. **This is a completely unoccupied position and it is upstream of everything else.**

## 🟠 7. MENTOR LOGISTICS AT SCALE — the compliance dimension nobody has

Mentorship is universally a directory + calendar. But in a university/IIC context, mentors are:
- **Often external, and therefore subject to conflict-of-interest declaration, NDA, and IP non-disclosure** before they see any pre-incubation idea
- Required to log attendance and hours **against a scheme's utilisation rules** (NIDHI/BIRAC/CSR fund mentors' time)
- Required to **sign a confidentiality agreement before seeing innovations** — precisely what AICTE's own YUKTI-NIR NDA templates address ("PATENT / INVENTION NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT")
- Subject to **conflict checks against their own IP/startup holdings**
- Needing **utilisation dashboards** ("Prof. X mentored 40 startups, 68 hours, ₹X of scheme-funded mentor cost")

No platform separates "public mentor" from "confidential expert under NDA" visibility tiers, or logs mentorship as a fundable scheme cost. **A single NDA leakage could void an IPR claim.** This is a real institutional fear and nobody has solved it.

## 11. 🔴 THE CRITICAL ANALYTICAL QUESTION

### *"What is the structural gap between what Indian institutions are REQUIRED to report (IIC/YUKTI/NIRF/AISHE/DPIIT) and what software actually EXISTS to help them manage startups day-to-day?"*

#### The one-sentence answer

**Every mandated system is a periodic compliance ledger keyed on *events and snapshots*; not one of them is a system of record keyed on a *venture through time*.** The gap is not missing features inside an existing product — it is a category error: India digitised innovation **accountability** and never digitised innovation **operations**.

---

### Gap 1 — The unit of record is wrong. This is the root cause.

| System | Unit of record |
|---|---|
| IIC | **The activity/event.** 16+ events/yr. Each = a form with 2 photos, a video URL, participant counts, one expenditure line. |
| YUKTI | **The innovation snapshot.** Three static buckets — Idea/PoC (TRL 1-4), Innovation/Prototype (TRL 5-9), Business Model/Startup. |
| NIRF-I | **The spreadsheet row.** Name + DPIIT no. + amount + year. |
| AISHE | **The institution-year aggregate.** |
| DPIIT | **The recognition certificate.** |

**What does not exist in any of them: the venture entity with state over time.** There is no record anywhere that says: *venture V was founded by student S from batch 2023; entered incubation 2024-06; raised ₹40 L from investor I in 2025-02 at valuation ₹X; is now at Series A; founder has taken 12/24 of 24 months of vesting; next milestone is X on date Y.*

Consequence: **an institution literally cannot answer "how many of our incubatees are still alive?" from any government system.** IIC's "Overall Impact" is *students and faculty benefited from activities* — event attendance, not venture survival.

---

### Gap 2 — No operational startup management anywhere in the mandated stack

Enumerated against the actual portal menus (§2.6) and YUKTI/NIRF screens (§3, §4.3):

| Capability | IIC | YUKTI | NIRF-I | AISHE | DPIIT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Venture pipeline / CRM stages | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Cohort intake & graduation | ✗ (registers the *centre*) | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Equity / vesting / founder IP transfer | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Fundraising pipeline | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Runway / burn / cap-table | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| IPR docket, deadlines, renewals | ✗ (directory of the *centre*) | ✗ | annual counts only | ✗ | ✗ |
| Mentor session mgmt | form only | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | via MAARG, startup-scoped |
| Compliance calendar for the startup | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Seat / space allocation | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Institutional benchmarking vs peers | aggregate only | ✗ | rank only | ✗ | ✗ |

**The single sharpest illustration:** IIC has a menu item called **"IPR/TTO Centres."** An institution with a working technology-transfer office — filing patents, managing renewals, licensing — registers that office's *existence and floor area* in IIC. **The portal then has no place to record a single patent.** Meanwhile NIRF-I asks for "Patents Commercialized & Technology Transferred" and "Income from Technology Transferred (in Rupees)" — so the institution maintains a **separate, manual, off-platform patent ledger** purely to answer that one NIRF screen. ## 12. Strategic implications for StartupHub

1. **Position as the write-side system of record, not another reporting portal.** Government is building the *registry*; nobody is building the *operating system*.
2. **The single canonical entity is the venture** — with a startup master record that carries `DIPP no.` as its first-class external key. This is the join key to NIRF, YUKTI, KSUM UID, state portals and (eventually) BHASKAR.
3. **Compliance must be a by-product, not a feature.** If StartupHub's users log activities, mentor sessions, IPR filings and venture milestones for *operational* reasons, the IIC/NIRF/YUKTI/ARIIA submissions become generated reports. That is the entire value proposition — and it is the exact thing no mandated platform does.
4. **Respect the hard gates when designing.** The IIC council-meeting→activity dependency and the MIC-driven-mandatory list are real constraints. A naive sync will fail.
5. **Model the Feb 2026 recognition change.** Standard vs Deep Tech bifurcation (₹200cr/10yr vs ₹300cr/20yr) plus cooperative societies. Historical YUKTI/NIRF records predate this.
6. **Do not compete with MAARG for mentorship workflow** — MAARG already does session goals, reports and outcome tracking well. Instead, integrate: StartupHub holds the *institution-side* context MAARG cannot see (which student, which cohort, which incubation seat, which grant consumed).
7. **Build the institutional reconciliation layer.** No cross-portal canonical ID exists. Whoever maintains the mapping — Institute (AISHE code) ↔ NIRF institute_id ↔ ARIIA ID ↔ IIC ID ↔ Venture (DIPP) — owns the ecosystem graph.
8. **Commission primary research on burden.** No published literature exists (§10). A field study of 20 IIC institutions measuring hours per submission across IIC + NIRF-I + YUKTI + ARIIA + AISHE + state portal would be a defensible, citable, first-in-India artefact — and a strong wedge.

# CRITICAL OUTPUT

## "WHY HASN'T THE PROBLEM BEEN SOLVED?"

For each strong global player — the specific thing that stops them from serving Indian HEIs at Indian price points.

### 1. AcceleratorApp — *the* closest competitor
> **"It replaces the five tools your team is already patching together. From the first application to the final portfolio report."** — $499/mo, 200+ orgs, MIT Design X and Yale as customers.

**What stops them:**
- **Their product is designed around a *funding institution*, not an institution.** The presence of a separate "Funding" module with **capital call management** and **disbursement tranches** ([acceleratorapp.co/en/pricing/funding](https://www.acceleratorapp.co/en/pricing/funding)) tells you the unit of sale is a government or corporate venture fund dispensing money. An Indian HEI *receives* DST grants (NIDHI-CoE, NIDHI-PRAYAS, TBI) but **disburses none to student startups** — that money comes from DST or the institution. The hardest, most differentiated half of their product has no Indian HEI use case.
- **No Indian regulatory reporting at all.** ARIIA, NIDHI utilisation reports, MoE Innovation Cell calendar filings, YUKTI repository feeds, DPIIT recognition tracking. StartupHub's central reason to exist does not exist for them. Their customers report to *their own funders*, not to a ministry.
- **Audit logs and SSO are Enterprise-only, quote-gated.** An HEI whose MoE-mandated IIC structure requires demonstrable governance cannot self-serve it. Meanwhile their own blog says: *"For a single, small program, yes, [a Google Sheet] can work fine… The problem starts when programs grow"* — i.e. the Indian tier-2/3 college segment is *below* their economic threshold.
- **Pricing scales with concurrent programs and seats** ($49/Funding seat, $99/additional program) — exactly the per-seat dynamic Indian institutions distrust.
- **16-person company, New York.** No Indian entity, no Indian GST invoicing, no Indian-language-first UX, no on-ground implementation capacity, no India data residency story. Their strongest Indian-adjacent proof point is German Accelerator (a German government programme).

### 2. Elsevier Pure
> The most institutionally-credible system in the world. 500+ universities, 50+ countries. Public price list.

**What stops them:**
- **Price is structurally disqualifying.** Even the smallest tier is **£19,406/yr ≈ ₹26 lakh** for the Core module alone, with **£13,500 + £18,750 mandatory one-off fees** and **£5,188 per integration**. For an Indian college this is not a purchase decision; it is a non-starter requiring a research grant to justify.
- **Wrong object model.** Pure manages research outputs, publications, awards and grants. It has **no concept of a startup, a cohort, a mentor match, an incubation milestone, or a demo day.** An IIC does not need a CRIS; it needs a startup portfolio. The overlap is a shared IT department, nothing more.
- **Indian institutional budgets are built for teaching, not research infrastructure.** The target user (research office, librarian, VP Research) does not exist or does not have budget authority at a typical Indian private college.
- **Upmarket/European-first.** Even at Tier 1, the price assumes a university with 1,000+ researchers and a functioning IT integration team.

### 3. IdeaScale
> The only innovation-management vendor with a genuinely public price list — and it starts at **$12,999 for two years**.

**What stops them:**
- **The floor is at the ceiling of StartupHub's band** ($6,500/yr ≈ ₹5.85L) **before** any module is added, and the vendor now describes pricing as "custom subscriptions" with no published list ([pricingsaas.com](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/ideascale)).
- **Their model is campaign/crowdsourcing-shaped** — votes, gamification, community participation, 4.5M participants. An IIC needs a **structured, defensible record** for a ministry submission. Crowd-engagement mechanics are the opposite of an auditable register.
- **Independence problem.** Their published evaluation scores **reporting 2.4/5** and **single source of truth 1.9/5** — the two dimensions that matter most to a HEI buying for ARIIA/NIDHI compliance ([itonics-innovation.com](https://www.itonics-innovation.com/blog/top-innovation-management-software), 15 Oct 2026).
- **Education is named as a vertical but never as the buyer.** FedRAMP and SSO are aimed at government, not Indian HEIs.

### 4. Devpost
> The reason Indian student hackathons already work.

**What stops them:**
- **It is free *because* it is only a hackathon layer.** Free for student orgs hosting student-only events, academic-email verified ([help.devpost.com/article/127](https://help.devpost.com/article/127-how-do-i-create-a-hackathon)). Enterprise/custom is *"reported to run from the low five figures to $80K depending on scope"* ([builderbase.com/compare/devpost](https://www.builderbase.com/compare/devpost/); [hackathonhost.com](https://hackathonhost.com/articles/hackathon-platform-pricing-comparison)).
- **It stops at the hackathon.** No cohort management, no pre-incubation pipeline, no incubation portfolio, no IPR, no ministry reporting. A college runs SIH on Devpost and then has *nothing* for the rest of the year.
- **It is a US company with no Indian regulatory or language layer.** No ARIIA, no NIDHI, no GST.
- **Strategically, this is StartupHub's clearest wedge:** Devpost owns the event. Nobody owns what happens after it.

### 5. Airtable / Notion / Coda
> Cheap, flexible, already adopted.

**What stops them:**
- **Audit log, SCIM, SAML, SOC 2, HIPAA, granular RBAC, row-level permissions and compliance all sit above the tier an Indian college will buy.** Notion's audit log *"events are recorded starting from the time of upgrade"* — no retroactive history ([notion.com/help/audit-log](https://www.notion.com/help/audit-log)).
- **Airtable's quota walls are the wall**: 25,000 automation runs and 100,000 API calls/month on Team; automation limits consume quota even when switched off ([superdupr.com](https://superdupr.com/blog/airtable-pricing)). An institution running annual cohort + ARIIA + grant reporting will hit them.
- **Neither has referential integrity**, which is precisely what a startup↔IPR↔mentor↔funding model needs.
- **No single price a budget holder can plan against** beyond a seat count — and per-seat models are the exact thing Indian institutions distrust (see §4 ERP evidence).
- **No ministry reporting, ever.**

### 6. Indian Campus ERPs
> Already installed, already paid for, already trusted.

**What stops them:**
- **Nobody has built the module and nobody wants to.** AskERP gets closest — "publication tracker, grant management, H-index" — but there is no startup portfolio, no IIC workflow, no mentorship, no incubation anywhere in the Indian ERP module lists surveyed (Academia, Instita, MasterSoft, DigiERP, OnCampusERP, EduplusCampus, AskERP).
- **Their incentive structure is wrong.** ERP vendors make money from *more modules and more seats*. StartupHub's model (flat fee, low price, narrow scope) is antagonistic to their revenue model.
- **Their buyer is the Principal/Registrar.** The IIC nodal officer — StartupHub's actual buyer — has no seat in that buying decision.
- **Per-student pricing actively punishes the very thing ARIIA rewards** (a college that incubates more startups).

### 7. Anaqua / CPA Global / PatSnap / LexisNexis IP
> Real IP-management depth.

**What stops them:**
- **Enterprise IP docket economics don't exist at an Indian college scale.** A college files maybe 20–200 patents/year (PDEU IIC reports "140+ IPR filed" cumulatively — [pdeu.ac.in](https://pdeu.ac.in/academics/centre-of-excellence/innovation-and-incubation-centre)). A global docketing platform is built for thousands of matters and dozens of attorneys.
- **PatSnap's real entry contract is $15k–$30k/yr** — 2–5× the top of StartupHub's band — before modules ([beyondelevation.com](https://beyondelevation.com/insights/patsnap-pricing-2026)).
- **Patent analytics is not the job.** An IIC's job is *tracking filings and nudging students to file*, not landscaping competitors.
- **Anaqua acquired Unified Patents in Aug 2026 and Patrix before that** — consolidating at the enterprise end, moving further from small institutions.

## THE 5 STRUCTURAL REASONS THE INDIAN INSTITUTIONAL MARKET IS UNDER-SERVED

### 1. **The buyer has no budget line, and the budget is compliance-shaped, not feature-shaped.**
The IIC Nodal Officer is the user, but the money comes from an IIC grant, an NIDHI-TBI/CoE grant, or an Atal Lab. India's entire compliance discourse runs the other way: *"a typical firm has to navigate around 1,500 Acts which collectively impose 69,233 separate compliances and 6,618 independent filings"* (PRS Standing Committee summary, 7 Aug 2026, [prsindia.org](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/doing-business-in-india-the-way-forward)). **The only pitch that lands is "stop re-keying the same data into four places."** Feature marketing fails; time-and-risk elimination works. No global vendor has made this pitch because it is only true in India.

### 2. **The price gap is a cliff, not a slope — and it sits 10–100× below every incumbent's floor.**
| | Floor price | vs StartupHub band (₹50k–5L) |
|---|---|---|
| AcceleratorApp | $499/mo = ₹5.4L/yr | Top edge |
| Devpost (enterprise) | ~₹9L–₹72L per event | 2–14× above |
| IdeaScale | $12,999/2yr = ₹5.85L/yr | Top edge |
| Elsevier Pure | £19,406/yr = ₹26L/yr | **5× above** |
| Sphera (analogue) | $268,953/yr = ₹2.4Cr/yr | **485× above** |
| Anaqua / CPA Global / PatSnap | Quote-only enterprise | Far above |
| **Notion Plus, 8 seats** | **₹86k/yr** | **Inside the band** |
| **Trello Standard, 10 users** | **₹54k/yr** | **At the floor** |
| **WhatsApp + Sheets** | **₹0** | **Free** |

There is **no global vendor occupying ₹50k–5L for institutional startup management.** The two nearest price points (Notion Plus, Trello Standard) are generic work tools without audit, compliance or ministry reporting. **The gap is not "no one has built it" — it is "no one has built it *at this price with Indian compliance baked in*."**

### 3. **The demand is mandated but the data model is not standardised — so no global vendor can template it.**
The MoE/AICTE IIC framework fixes the *organisational* shape — President, Convenor, Innovation Activity Coordinator, Startup Activity Coordinator, IPR Coordinator, Social Media Coordinator, ARIIA/NIRF Coordinators ([dayanandasagar.edu IIC charter](https://www.dayanandasagar.edu/dsit/images/pdf/IICa.pdf)) — but each institution invents its own fields, its own mentor taxonomy, its own milestone vocabulary. ARIIA, NIDHI-PRAYAS, YUKTI, SIH and DST each demand **different, overlapping but non-identical** submission formats, evidenced in the public IIT Mandi and IMS UC submissions. **Global platforms standardise the buyer (a corporation with a clean HR directory and a chart of accounts). There is no such thing as a standard Indian HEI. The only way to productise this is to absorb the reporting formats as a first-class requirement — a purely Indian, purely local cost.**

### 4. **Every workaround is free, and the true cost is invisible — so the problem is chronically under-articulated.**
Excel, Google Sheets, Google Sites and WhatsApp are all ₹0, and all are genuinely adequate at the scale of a single hackathon or a 30-startup cohort. Nobody is *complaining*, because nothing is broken — the process is merely expensive in a currency (nodal-officer hours and re-keying) that no software purchase is ever benchmarked against. AcceleratorApp's own diagnosis is the clearest statement of this: *"The tools most accelerators are using weren't built for this… Strong applicants get missed because a reviewer's scores never made it into the master sheet"* ([acceleratorapp.co blog, 30 Apr 2026](https://www.acceleratorapp.co/en/blogs/why-accelerators-struggle-application-management-software/)). **The incumbent does not lose a single user — it just quietly consumes the institution's most constrained human resource.**

### 5. **The workforce is transient, so the incumbent's switching cost is artificially low — and the buyer's tenure is shorter than the payback period.**
The IIC operating unit is named *student* coordinators and rotating faculty (VIT Vellore's domain system; IISc's student coordinators; the IIC charter's "Innovation Activity Coordinator" and "Startup Activity Coordinator" roles are commonly student-held — [innovations.vit.ac.in](https://innovations.vit.ac.in/innovations/iic.html); [iic.iisc.ac.in/student-coordinators](https://iic.iisc.ac.in/student-coordinators)). **The person who would champion StartupHub graduates in 2–3 years; the person who signs is a faculty member who may transfer.** This creates three hard structural penalties: near-zero willingness to pay for migration services; an instinctive preference for a tool that is trivially learnable by a fresh coordinator each year; and **no institutional memory to accumulate in the incumbent**, which paradoxically means **there is little data to migrate — reducing switching friction to near zero**. A vendor must therefore win on *this year's* value proposition, and price below the point where it needs a business case.
