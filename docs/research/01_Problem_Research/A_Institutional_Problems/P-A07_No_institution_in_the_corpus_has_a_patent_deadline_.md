# P-A07 - No institution in the corpus has a patent deadline, and IP records are held outside the innovation record

**Evidence grade:** `DOCUMENTED` - Multiple primary documents read and cited by corpus_id.

**Severity:** `CRITICAL` - Compliance, legal or commercial risk if it goes wrong.

**Corpus documents cited:** `IIC-001`, `IIC-005`, `IIC-016`, `IIC-015`, `IIC-011`

> **Not an observed user problem.** Phase 3 user interviews have not been conducted. This is documentary analysis of what institutions publish about themselves. Do not present it to a third party as an observed user problem.

---

## Statement

IP activity is tracked as counts of filings, publications and grants. Nothing observed tracks prosecution deadlines, ownership, or the link between an invention and the innovation that produced it.

## Evidence

- NIT Calicut, the richest IP record in the corpus: 75 applications filed, 36 granted, 39 published, 10 in process - reported as counts only. No deadline, no next-action date, no attorney, no fee. (`IIC-001`)
- Peer-reviewed work on university TTO practice analysed 382 patent records and found poor data cleaning carries legal and commercialisation consequences. (`ACAD-001`)
- Agricultural-university TTO literature documents that missed prosecution deadlines produce abandoned applications - the entire patent is the cost of a missing date field. (`ACAD-002`)
- Faculty file patents independently of their institution, which prevents the institution claiming NIRF/ARIIA credit for work done on its own campus. (`ACAD-003`)
- A mandatory IP register at KIT CoE already carries a Yes/No column: 'Has this IP been Commercialized/Transferred to Industry/Startup/Society?' - proving the field is understood and wanted, yet absent from the IIC form. (`IIC-015`)
- Institute of Science Nagpur's flagship innovation is a UK design registration (No. 6381983). No field in the observed reporting vocabulary accommodates foreign IP. (`IIC-011`)

### What is still missing

The academic sources are Tier 2 and were located but not read in full this session; ACAD-001/002/003 need direct citation before external use. No corpus document shows a deadline field, which is an absence finding - consistent across 36 documents, but not proof that no institution has one internally.

## Who experiences it

IPR officer / TTO in-charge (rare and named at only 2 of 36 institutions - Manav Rachna's IPR Cell, and CMRIT's KSCST-collaborated IPR Cell); research scholars chasing grants; ARIIA assessors.

## Severity - CRITICAL

The only problem graded CRITICAL in Section A. A missed prosecution deadline extinguishes the patent; an unclaimed invention cannot be counted in NIRF or ARIIA. Both losses are permanent and both are documented consequences, not speculation.

## Frequency

Per patent, continuously, across multi-year prosecution timelines.

## Current workaround

Manual deadline tracking, if it happens at all. Two institutions publish a named IPR Cell with an in-charge and direct email (`IIC-005`, `IIC-016`); the other 34 publish no IP owner at all.

## Quantified cost

Not directly measured. The ACAD-002 literature establishes the consequence (abandoned application) without quantifying frequency in this population.

## Failure consequence

Permanent loss of the patent; permanent loss of the institution's claim to it for NIRF/ARIIA purposes; possible loss of funding attached to the invention.

## Potential fixer

An IP record with prosecution milestones and deadlines, linked to the invention, with ownership and a commercialisation-status field.

## Competitive implication

This is the strongest strategic finding in Phase 1 and it corroborates the Phase 0 thesis independently. Phase 0 found, across 11 direct competitors, that NOT ONE has an IPR module - only a data field in a Swedish product and a dashboard tile in IncubatX. The documentary corpus now shows why that gap persists: institutions track IP as counts inside a form that cannot hold a lifecycle.
