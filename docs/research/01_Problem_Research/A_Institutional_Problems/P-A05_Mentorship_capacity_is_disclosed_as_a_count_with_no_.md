# P-A05 - Mentorship capacity is disclosed as a count with no ratio, workload or outcome

**Evidence grade:** `SINGLE-SOURCE` - One primary document. Suggestive; must not carry a claim alone.

**Severity:** `MEDIUM` - Recurring friction that slows work but is survivable.

**Corpus documents cited:** `IIC-001`, `IIC-024`, `IIC-025`, `IIC-010`

> **Not an observed user problem.** Phase 3 user interviews have not been conducted. This is documentary analysis of what institutions publish about themselves. Do not present it to a third party as an observed user problem.

---

## Statement

Institutions report how many mentors they have. None report mentor capacity per innovator, hours contributed, or any mentorship outcome.

## Evidence

- NIT Calicut: 29 faculty mentors against 155 ideas generated in the year - roughly 5.3 ideas per mentor, with no indication of mentor hours or whether all 155 were mentored. (`IIC-001`)
- SSCET: 40 IIC members, 30 innovation ambassadors, and 2 faculty mentors/mentees. (`IIC-024`)
- Vijaya Institute of Pharmaceutical Sciences: 5 faculty mentors, 12 IIC members (12 faculty, 13 students), 12 innovation ambassadors of whom 10 were still in training. (`IIC-025`)
- BIT Mesra is the only institution publishing a mentorship-linked money figure: INR 1 lakh per student team, 7 teams. It still publishes no session count or outcome. (`IIC-010`)

### What is still missing

Only 4 of 36 documents publish any mentor count at all, and none publish workload or outcome. The mentor-count-vs-idea ratio spans 2.1 to 20 across these four, which may reflect real capacity differences or nothing more than differing disclosure habits. NOT sufficient to claim institutions under-manage mentorship. To close: Phase 3 interviews plus a wider mentor-field extraction across the corpus.

## Who experiences it

IIC coordinator (allocating mentors); mentors themselves; students requesting mentorship.

## Severity - MEDIUM

Evidence is thin and the failure mode is diffuse. Not a HIGH-severity claim until mentorship outcome data exists.

## Frequency

Continuous during the academic year.

## Current workaround

UNKNOWN from documents. Likely informal allocation via email and WhatsApp, but that is an expectation and is flagged as such.

## Quantified cost

Not available.

## Failure consequence

Cannot be stated on this evidence. A plausible risk is mentor double-booking and students receiving no response, but that is reasoning, not observation.

## Potential fixer

Mentor capacity and session logging against innovation objects, so allocation is visible and mentor time is auditable.

## Competitive implication

Phase 0 noted that no competitor separates a public mentor from a confidential expert under NDA. If mentorship proves to be a managed object in Phase 3, that gap becomes relevant. Do not claim it yet.
