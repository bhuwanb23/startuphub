# P-A01 - Institutions cannot produce a defensible list of their own startups

**Evidence grade:** `DOCUMENTED` - Multiple primary documents read and cited by corpus_id.

**Severity:** `HIGH` - Recurring, substantial time loss or error rate.

**Corpus documents cited:** `IIC-001`, `IIC-001b`, `IIC-032`, `IIC-007`

> **Not an observed user problem.** Phase 3 user interviews have not been conducted. This is documentary analysis of what institutions publish about themselves. Do not present it to a third party as an observed user problem.

---

## Statement

An institution cannot state, from its own records, which ventures it incubated, who founded them, or whether they still exist. The startup figure that reaches the government is unreconcilable with the institution's own understanding of it.

## Evidence

- NIT Calicut's single 2024-25 report states 55 startups in its narrative - broken down as 13 student/alumni, 4 faculty and 38 'others' - and 17 in its IIC-portal KPI form. 3.2x divergence inside one PDF. (`IIC-001`, `IIC-001b`)
- The 38 'others' category is itself an admission: 69% of the incubated population cannot be attributed to students, faculty or any defined owner. (`IIC-001`)
- Brainware University reports 91 patents published, 8 granted and 0 startups - an institution that demonstrably produced research output but reports no venture at all. (`IIC-032`)
- ICFAI University Tripura reports startups established as 0 and startups in pipeline as 5, the only institution in the corpus that separates the two. (`IIC-007`)

### What is still missing

Whether institutions keep ANY internal venture register is unknown. A corpus that reports zero startups may have zero, or may simply not be counting. Only an interview can separate those.

## Who experiences it

IIC coordinator; Director-Research or R&D office; anyone answering a board, AICTE inspection or ARIIA question about incubated ventures.

## Severity - HIGH

Not CRITICAL - no evidence in this corpus of a legal or licensing consequence. But it is the single hardest number to defend, and it is asked for by inspection bodies.

## Frequency

Annual and unconditional. Every institution must answer it every year, and cannot.

## Current workaround

Reconstructing the number from memory, from incubator records, from DPIIT recognitions, and from Udyam registrations held by founders - then reconciling those sources by hand before submitting.

## Quantified cost

Not measured. No source in this corpus quantifies coordinator time. Available proxy only: the divergence between the two NIT Calicut figures is 38 ventures of unexplained difference, which is the size of the reconciliation problem, not the cost of it.

## Failure consequence

An institution either over-claims (and is corrected during inspection, damaging credibility and future funding) or under-claims (and loses ARIIA points it earned). A startup the institution actually incubated can end up unclaimed and unattributed.

## Potential fixer

A venture register that is the institution's system of record, with founder, cohort, incorporation date and current status - exporting to the government screens rather than replacing them.

## Competitive implication

None of the 11 direct competitors surveyed in Phase 0 model a venture-with-a-lifecycle as the primary object; they track incubation intake and programme milestones. A venture register with status over time is a direct differentiator, not a feature.
