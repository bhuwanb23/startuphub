## The five corpus findings, briefly

1. **`ideas generated` is not one measurement.** Implied idea-to-prototype conversion spans 0.0% to 262.5% across 19 institutions; one exceeds 100%, which is impossible for a same-year funnel.
2. **Six same-document contradictions.** NIT Calicut's single PDF reports both 55 and 17 startups, and both Rs 1.51 lakh and Rs 16 lakh awareness spend.
3. **No controlled vocabulary for absence.** At least seven ways to write "nothing happened" in the same mandated form, including one numeric money field filled with a 13-item prose narrative.
4. **Grant money is often the wrong money.** One institution's "innovation grant" reconciles exactly to two unrelated DBT academic grants.
5. **Activity counts do not predict outcomes.** 94 activities produced zero IPs at one institution; 42 activities produced 843 ideas at another.

## The critical limitation

**No user interviews have been conducted.** Phase 3 has not started. Everything in Phase 1 is documentary analysis of documents institutions published about themselves. That is real evidence about reporting behaviour. It is not evidence about what users say they struggle with, and it must not be presented as such.

The most important open question created by this corpus is internal tooling: no retrieved document reveals what sits *upstream* of the IIC form. We have only ever seen the export. Until Phase 3 establishes it, todo.md Section B's "Excel dependency" claims remain unevidenced.
