## The headline: the product is cheap, and nearly unbuyable

Both halves are evidenced, and together they redefine the commercial problem.

### 1. Affordability has never been the problem

A NIT's FY 2025-26 allocation averages **Rs 1,89,58,23,333** (Rs 190 crore).

Our list price is **Rs 1,20,000**.

> ### That is 0.0063% of the institution's entire annual budget.

Not once, not anywhere, in any segment, have we been price-disqualified. **Any go-to-market plan that leads with price is solving the wrong problem.**

### 2. The money is already unspent

NIT budget **utilisation fell to 67%** in FY 2023-24 — the lowest since FY 2019-20. That is **Rs 53,03,00,000 unspent per NIT**.

| Group | Utilisation | Unspent | Per institution | Our price as % of unspent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| NITs and IIEST | 67% | Rs 1,591 cr | Rs 53.03 cr | 0.0008% |
| Indian Institutes of Technology | 84% | Rs 1,661 cr | Rs 72.23 cr | 0.0007% |
| Central Universities | 90% | Rs 1,239 cr | Rs 26.94 cr | 0.0010% |
| Other autonomous bodies | 96% | Rs 220 cr | Rs 22.00 cr | 0.0054% |

### 3. So the barrier is process

Phase 2 already found the real blockers:

- a **20× spread** in committee triggers across 8 institutional instruments (Rs 50,000 to Rs 10,00,000)
- **zero of the twelve** mandated IIC seats hold documented budget authority

> **A product that is 0.006% of the budget, sitting behind a procurement committee that 0 of 12 authorised officers can bypass, is a process problem.** Reducing friction is the whole game.

## The state-public system — 81% of students, barely in this budget

*Source: NITI Aayog, 'Expanding Quality Higher Education through States and State Public Universities', Feb 2025*

- **495 State Public Universities** with **46,000 affiliated institutions** carry **81%** of all student enrolment (3.25 crore students).
- They are funded by **state governments**, so they barely appear in the Union budget.
- The only central line reaching them is **PM-USHA: Rs 1,815 crore**.
- Higher education expenditure is **0.62% of GDP**; total education 4.64% against an NEP target of 6.0%.

**This is the real college market** — not the 45,473 AISHE figure, which counts registration. 46,000 institutions, 81% of the students, and essentially invisible in the financial data.

## What this does NOT license

> **Headroom is not willingness.** Rs 53 crore unspent per NIT is *capacity* evidence. Nothing in the public record establishes what any institution would *pay*. Conflating the two would be the same inference error this repository has refused throughout.

**Phase 7's price band (Rs 75,000–1,20,000) is deliberately left unchanged** on the strength of unspent budget. The correct use of this data is to know we have room to move — not to justify moving yet. That requires `OQ-03`.
